Polyendocrine Metabolic Ovarian Syndrome (PMOS)

If you or someone you love has ever been diagnosed with Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS), or if you suspect you might have it, there is a major medical update you need to know. Health experts worldwide have officially renamed this common condition to Polyendocrine Metabolic Ovarian Syndrome (PMOS).

This condition affects about 1 in 8 women globally, making proper care and understanding absolutely essential. Here at Maria Hospital in Kathmandu, Nepal, we want to walk you through what this new name means, how it impacts your body, and how routine checkups can help you manage symptoms for a healthier and happier life.

Why Did the Name Change?

For years, the term "PCOS" caused a lot of unnecessary stress for patients. It made it sound like the root problem was dangerous cysts growing on the ovaries. In reality, these "cysts" are simply natural, harmless egg follicles that never fully developed. Many women with the condition do not even get them.

Because the old name put so much focus on the ovaries, it often delayed the right diagnosis and care. The new name, PMOS, clears up this confusion. It highlights that this is actually a complex hormonal and metabolic issue that affects the whole body, which ultimately leads to much better treatment options.

What Does PMOS Actually Mean?

The new name breaks down exactly what is going on in your body into three main areas:

  • Polyendocrine: This means multiple hormone systems are involved, including insulin and androgens, which are natural hormones present in all women.
  • Metabolic: The condition changes how your body handles energy. This can lead to struggles with weight management, insulin resistance, and a higher risk of type 2 diabetes.
  • Ovarian: Your reproductive system is still deeply affected, which can impact your menstrual cycle, ovulation, and overall fertility.

Common Symptoms to Watch For

Because PMOS impacts multiple systems, it can look very different from person to person. A few of the most common signs include:

  • Irregular or missing periods.
  • Excess hair growth on the face, chest, or back.
  • Stubborn acne or excessively oily skin.
  • Thinning hair on the scalp.
  • Sudden weight gain or a really hard time losing weight.
  • Dark, thick patches of skin, usually on the neck, groin, or under the breasts.
  • Difficulty getting pregnant (infertility).
  • Mental health struggles like anxiety, depression, and constant fatigue.

Protecting Your Long-Term Health

PMOS is not just about fertility. It is a lifelong condition. If left unmanaged, the hormonal and metabolic imbalances can raise your risk for serious health issues down the road, including high blood pressure, high cholesterol, type 2 diabetes, heart disease, and sleep apnea.

That is exactly why scheduling a doctor's visit sooner rather than later is so important. Getting care early is the best way to protect your future well-being.

How is PMOS Diagnosed?

During a routine health checkup, your doctor will look over your medical history and talk with you about your symptoms. Getting a proper diagnosis usually comes down to finding at least two of the following three signs:

  • Irregular Cycles: A history of missed, irregular, or unusually heavy periods.
  • High Hormone Levels: Physical signs of excess androgens like acne or extra body hair, or a blood test that confirms your hormone levels are high.
  • Ovarian Changes: An ultrasound showing enlarged ovaries with lots of small follicles, or a specific blood test revealing high AMH levels.

Comprehensive Treatment at Maria Hospital

While there is no permanent cure for PMOS right now, the symptoms are incredibly manageable. At Maria Hospital, we build personalized treatment plans that fit your unique body and lifestyle goals. Your care plan might include:

  • Healthy Lifestyle Changes: Eating a balanced diet and staying active are the best first steps for managing PMOS. Losing just 5% to 10% of your body weight can vastly improve your periods, lower your diabetes risk, and boost your chances of getting pregnant.
  • Hormonal Birth Control: If you are not looking to get pregnant right now, birth control pills or hormonal IUDs can help regulate your cycle, protect your uterus, and clear up your skin.
  • Insulin and Weight Medications: Drugs like Metformin help your body process blood sugar better and can assist in regulating your periods. We might also look into newer weight-management medications to help treat insulin resistance.
  • Anti-Androgen Medicines: These medications block the effects of excess hormones to reduce severe acne and unwanted facial or body hair.
  • Fertility Support: If you are ready to grow your family, PMOS is one of the most treatable causes of infertility.
